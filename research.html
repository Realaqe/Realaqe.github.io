Research

I measure the masses and orbits of giant planets and brown dwarfs. Most substellar companions are seen only indirectly, as a wobble in their host star's radial velocity or position on the sky, and each technique alone leaves the mass or orbital orientation uncertain. By fitting Hipparcos and Gaia astrometry, radial velocities, and direct imaging together, I obtain model-independent dynamical masses and full three-dimensional orbits, and use them to study how these objects form and evolve.

Orbits and masses from Hipparcos–Gaia astrometry

The 25-year baseline between Hipparcos and Gaia reveals the slow acceleration a long-period companion induces on its host star. Combined with radial velocities, it breaks the mass–inclination degeneracy of radial velocities alone. I fit orbits and masses for a sample of 156 companions this way and used the results to test the orbital solutions in Gaia's Non-Single Star catalog. This independent check matters for interpreting the much larger exoplanet yield expected from the next Gaia data release.

  • An, Q., Brandt, T. D., Brandt, G. M., & Venner, A. 2025, ApJS, 280, 61 — Orbits and Masses for 156 Companions from Combined Astrometry and Radial Velocities, and a Validation of Gaia Non-single-star Solutions
  • Venner, A., An, Q., et al. 2024, MNRAS, 535, 90 — HD 28185 Revisited: An Outer Planet, Instead of a Brown Dwarf, on a Saturn-like Orbit

Three-dimensional architectures of planetary systems

Knowing a companion's full orbital orientation lets us ask how a system is tilted, not just how massive its planets are. In HAT-P-11, I combined astrometry and radial velocities with the stellar spin to show that the orbits of both planets are significantly inclined, relative to each other and to the star. In a companion paper, we traced this geometry to an early history of planet–planet scattering followed by von Zeipel–Lidov–Kozai migration.

  • An, Q., Lu, T., Brandt, G. M., Brandt, T. D., & Li, G. 2025, AJ, 169, 22 — Significant Mutual Inclinations Between the Stellar Spin and the Orbits of Both Planets in the HAT-P-11 System
  • Lu, T., An, Q., Li, G., et al. 2025, ApJ, 979, 218 — Planet–Planet Scattering and von Zeipel–Lidov–Kozai Migration—The Dynamical History of HAT-P-11

Finding and weighing companions with direct imaging

Astrometric accelerations point to stars likely to host a massive companion, and high-contrast imaging can then catch the companion directly. I observe with Keck/NIRC2 and Subaru/SCExAO-CHARIS as part of the OASIS survey and fit the resulting relative astrometry jointly with Gaia and radial-velocity data. This approach yielded a brown dwarf around the nearby M dwarf LSPM J1446+4633 (JHU news) and two new substellar companions from OASIS (Subaru press release). I am also a Co-I on JWST Cycle 5 GO 10758.

  • Uyama, T., Kuzuhara, M., Beichman, C., …, An, Q., et al. 2025, AJ, 170, 272 — Direct Imaging Explorations for Companions from the Subaru/IRD Strategic Program II; Discovery of a Brown-dwarf Companion around a Nearby Mid-M-dwarf LSPM J1446+4633

Software

  • orvara — Orbit fitting that jointly models radial velocities, relative astrometry, and Hipparcos–Gaia absolute astrometry. I am a co-developer and maintainer.
  • htof — Reproduces Hipparcos and Gaia intermediate astrometric data so that orbit fits can use them. I am a co-developer and maintainer.
